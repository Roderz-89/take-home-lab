How two loans are deducted

If you have an undergraduate plan and a Postgraduate Loan, your employer takes both from the same pay at once: 9% of pay above your plan’s threshold plus 6% of pay above £21,000. On £35,000 with Plan 2 that is £505.35 plus £840.00, so £1,345.35 a year. Holding two undergraduate plans is different: HMRC takes one 9% deduction above the lowest of their thresholds, so choose the plan with the lower threshold.     Why a payslip can differ from this result

    The calculator shows a whole tax year on a standard 1257L code. Real payroll works pay period by pay period. Sources: GOV.UK emergency tax codes, what your tax code means, HMRC rates and thresholds 2026 to 2027 and repaying your student loan (checked 17 October 2026).

    What if I am on an emergency tax code such as 1257L W1, 1257L M1, 0T or BR?

    A code ending in W1 or M1 (or X, or marked NONCUM on the payslip) is an emergency code: tax is worked out on that week’s or month’s pay alone, as if you were paid that amount all year. 0T gives no tax-free allowance at all, so on a £3,000 month you would pay about £209.50 more income tax than on 1257L. BR taxes everything from that job at the 20% basic rate and is usually used for a second job. In Scotland the same codes start with S, for example S1257L M1. This calculator always assumes a standard cumulative 1257L code. Emergency codes are usually corrected within about 35 days once HMRC has your details, and any overpaid tax comes back through payroll or as a refund.

    What is the difference between cumulative and week 1 / month 1 tax?

    On a normal cumulative code, each payslip looks at your pay and tax-free allowance for the whole tax year so far, so a month of overpaid or underpaid tax is corrected on the next payslip. On a week 1 or month 1 basis, each pay period is taxed on its own, with no catching up, so an error stays until your code changes or the tax year ends. Over a full year at steady pay the two usually give the same total, which is what this annual calculator shows.

    Why is my National Insurance different in a bonus or overtime month?

    Employee National Insurance is worked out on each pay period, not on the year. For monthly pay, 8% applies between £1,048 and £4,189 in that month and 2% above £4,189. On £35,000 a year with a £5,000 bonus paid in one month, NI is about £149 in a normal month and about £326 in the bonus month, because most of the bonus falls above the monthly upper limit and is charged at 2%. That is roughly £1,970 for the year, against £2,194.40 in this calculator’s annual model. See how a bonus is taxed.

    Are student loan repayments worked out per pay period too?

    Yes. Your employer compares each payslip with the weekly or monthly threshold, for example £2,448.75 a month for Plan 2, and rounds the deduction down to whole pounds. A month with a bonus or overtime can go over the threshold even if your yearly pay does not: on £24,000 a year with a £2,000 bonus in one month, that month has about £139 of Plan 2 deducted, although the annual model shows £0. You can ask the Student Loans Company for a refund if your income for the year ends up below the yearly threshold. For pay that changes week to week, see hourly pay to take-home.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does my payslip differ from this calculator?

    The calculator models a whole tax year on a standard 1257L tax code. Your payslip is worked out pay period by pay period, so a different or emergency tax code, a bonus month, overtime, benefits in kind or a different pension arrangement will move the figure. National Insurance and student loan repayments are also assessed each pay period rather than across the year. If the two disagree, trust the payslip and check your tax code in your HMRC personal tax account.

    How does salary sacrifice affect my pension?

    The salary you give up is paid into your pension by your employer as an employer contribution, so the pension receives the whole sacrificed amount with no income tax or National Insurance taken off first. Your cash salary is lower, which can reduce things worked out from salary, such as some life cover, mortgage affordability checks and statutory pay. The salary sacrifice guide compares it with a net pay pension.

    Does the calculator save or send what I type?

    No. The calculation runs in your browser using the published js/tax.js engine. Nothing you enter is sent to Rodway Labs or stored by the site.

    Which tax year and rates does it use?

    The 2026/27 tax year, 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Rates and thresholds are checked against HMRC and Scottish Government sources, listed on the methodology page.
