METHOD

How the calculator stacks a packet

Last checked 2 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27

Take-Home Lab runs an annual PAYE-style stack in the browser. Nothing you type is sent to Rodway Labs. The result is an illustration with the assumptions below, not a figure you can file or budget from without checking a real payslip.

Order of the stack

  1. Start from gross annual pay (salary, or hourly rate × hours × paid weeks).
  2. Apply pension treatment if selected: net pay reduces taxable pay only; salary sacrifice reduces taxable pay and NI-able earnings in this model.
  3. Income tax on taxable pay after the personal allowance (and Scottish bands where chosen).
  4. Employee Class 1 National Insurance on NI-able earnings for the year.
  5. One student loan plan, if selected, at 9% (or 6% postgraduate) above the plan threshold.
  6. Take-home = gross − tax − NI − loan − employee pension contribution in the model.

Income tax

England, Wales and Northern Ireland use the main rates: 20% on taxable pay up to £37,700, 40% to £125,140, 45% above. The standard personal allowance is £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000 (gone at £125,140). Scotland uses the 2026/27 Scottish bands on non-savings, non-dividend income. The model assumes a 1257L-style allowance unless the taper applies.

National Insurance

Employee Class 1 is modelled on the year: 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2%. Real payroll calculates National Insurance each pay period. A bonus month, short weeks or irregular overtime can therefore differ from this annual stack.

Student loans

One plan at a time. Plan 1, 2, 4 and 5 at 9% above the 2026/27 HMRC threshold. Postgraduate loan at 6% above £21,000. Plan 5 is modelled as the plan for new undergraduate loans issued from 6 April 2026 in England, threshold £25,000. The site does not combine two plans on one payslip.

Pension

A percentage of gross. Net pay reduces taxable pay, not NI, in this model. Salary sacrifice reduces both. Relief at source is not modelled. The employer booklet decides which design a scheme actually is.

Worked check — £35,000 England, no pension, no loan

  • Taxable pay after £12,570 allowance: £22,430
  • Income tax at 20%: £4,486.00
  • Employee NI at 8% on £22,430: £1,794.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £28,719.60 a year (£2,393.30 a month)

The same figures appear on the £35,000 take-home guide and the pay-rise guide. If your result differs, check region, pension and loan toggles first.

Left out

Other tax codes, marriage allowance, blind person’s allowance, the High Income Child Benefit Charge, savings and dividends, company benefits, court orders, payroll giving, Scottish starter/higher interactions beyond the published bands, and employer National Insurance.

Why a guide can disagree with a payslip

  • Your tax code is not 1257L, or you have a week-1 / month-1 basis.
  • National Insurance was calculated that pay period, not as an annual average.
  • You have two student loans, a postgraduate loan plus an undergraduate plan, or a different threshold.
  • Pension is salary sacrifice on the payslip but net pay in the model (or the reverse).
