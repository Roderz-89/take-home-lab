What’s £20k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

£20,000 is a typical full-time salary for entry-level and part-time-plus roles. Only £7,430 of it is above the personal allowance, so income tax and NI together take a small share. For comparison, a full-time job at the 2026/27 National Living Wage (£12.71 × 37.5 × 52) pays £24,784.50. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £20,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £20,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £7,430
  • Basic 20% on £7,430: £1,486.00
  • Income tax: £1,486.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£20,000 − £12,570) = £594.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £17,919.60 a year (£1,493.30 a month · £344.61 a week)

You keep 89.6% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 72p (20% tax and 8% NI).

Second example — £20,000 with a 5% net pay pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £20,000 gross · 5% net pay pension (£1,000.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £1,286.00 · employee NI: £594.40
  • Pension contribution: £1,000.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £17,119.60 a year (£1,426.63 a month)

Take-home falls by £800.00 while £1,000.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £200.00. A net pay scheme saves income tax only; salary sacrifice would also save NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £20k angle: low deductions, Scotland slightly cheaper

At £20,000 no undergraduate student loan is repaid: even Plan 5 starts at £25,000 (Plan 5 here: £0.00). A postgraduate loan does apply above £21,000, so not yet. In Scotland the 19% starter band makes income tax £1,446.33 instead of £1,486.00, so Scottish take-home is £39.67 a year higher (£17,959.27).

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.
