Take-home pay on £40,000 — Scotland

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

£40,000 has a large slice in the 21% intermediate band but stays below the Scottish higher rate at £43,663. National Insurance is the same everywhere in the UK, so the whole difference comes from income tax. Figures are for 2026/27 and come from the same engine as the calculator.

Scotland

  • Gross £40,000 · allowance £12,570 · taxable £27,430
  • Starter 19% on £3,967: £753.73
  • Basic 20% on £12,989: £2,597.80
  • Intermediate 21% on £10,474: £2,199.54
  • Scottish income tax: £5,551.07
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£40,000 − £12,570) = £2,194.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £32,254.53 a year (£2,687.88 a month)

Same salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland

  • Income tax: £5,486.00 · NI £2,194.40 — identical
  • Illustrated take-home: £32,319.60 a year (£2,693.30 a month)

The gap

Scottish income tax is £65.07 a year higher (about £5.42 a month). The 19% starter band saves £39.67 against the rest of the UK. The 21% intermediate band costs 1 point more than 20% on £10,474. At this salary a Scottish taxpayer’s next pound is taxed at 21%, plus 8% NI.

Second example — £40,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £40,000 gross · 5% salary sacrifice (£2,000.00) · no student loan.

  • Scottish income tax: £5,131.07 · NI £2,034.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £30,834.53 a year (£2,569.54 a month)
  • Rest of UK with the same pension: take-home £30,879.60

The pension costs £1,420.00 of Scottish take-home for £2,000.00 saved. Each £1 sacrificed saves 21p tax and 8p NI here.

With a Plan 4 student loan

Most Scottish graduates who studied in Scotland repay Plan 4: 9% above £33,795. At £40,000 that is 9% × £6,205 = £558.45 a year (£46.54 a month), leaving £31,696.08. See Plan 4 in Scotland.
