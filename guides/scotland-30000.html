Take-home pay on £30,000 — Scotland

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

£30,000 is just £473 into the Scottish 21% intermediate band, which starts at £29,527. National Insurance is the same everywhere in the UK, so the whole difference comes from income tax. Figures are for 2026/27 and come from the same engine as the calculator.

Scotland

  • Gross £30,000 · allowance £12,570 · taxable £17,430
  • Starter 19% on £3,967: £753.73
  • Basic 20% on £12,989: £2,597.80
  • Intermediate 21% on £474: £99.54
  • Scottish income tax: £3,451.07
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£30,000 − £12,570) = £1,394.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £25,154.53 a year (£2,096.21 a month)

Same salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland

  • Income tax: £3,486.00 · NI £1,394.40 — identical
  • Illustrated take-home: £25,119.60 a year (£2,093.30 a month)

The gap

Scottish income tax is £34.93 a year lower (about £2.91 a month). The 19% starter band saves £39.67 against the rest of the UK. The 21% intermediate band costs 1 point more than 20% on £474. At this salary a Scottish taxpayer’s next pound is taxed at 21%, plus 8% NI.

Second example — £30,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £30,000 gross · 5% salary sacrifice (£1,500.00) · no student loan.

  • Scottish income tax: £3,146.33 · NI £1,274.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £24,079.27 a year (£2,006.61 a month)
  • Rest of UK with the same pension: take-home £24,039.60

The pension costs £1,075.26 of Scottish take-home for £1,500.00 saved. Each £1 sacrificed saves 21p tax and 8p NI here.

With a Plan 4 student loan

Most Scottish graduates who studied in Scotland repay Plan 4: 9% above £33,795. At £30,000 nothing is repaid, because pay is below the threshold. See Plan 4 in Scotland.
