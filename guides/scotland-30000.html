Take-home pay on £30,000 — Scotland

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 17 October 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 17 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

Scope: Scottish taxpayers only, one salary. For the same salary elsewhere see £30,000 in the rest of the UK.

£30,000 is just £473 into the Scottish 21% intermediate band, which starts at £29,527. National Insurance is the same everywhere in the UK, so the whole difference comes from income tax. Figures are for 2026/27 and come from the same engine as the calculator.

Scotland

  • Gross £30,000 · allowance £12,570 · taxable £17,430
  • Starter 19% on £3,967: £753.73
  • Basic 20% on £12,989: £2,597.80
  • Intermediate 21% on £474: £99.54
  • Scottish income tax: £3,451.07
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£30,000 − £12,570) = £1,394.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £25,154.53 a year (£2,096.21 a month)

Same salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland

  • Income tax: £3,486.00 · NI £1,394.40 — identical
  • Illustrated take-home: £25,119.60 a year (£2,093.30 a month)

The gap

Scottish income tax is £34.93 a year lower (about £2.91 a month). The 19% starter band saves £39.67 against the rest of the UK. The 21% intermediate band costs 1 point more than 20% on £474. At this salary a Scottish taxpayer’s next pound is taxed at 21%, plus 8% NI.

Second example — £30,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £30,000 gross · 5% salary sacrifice (£1,500.00) · no student loan.

  • Scottish income tax: £3,146.33 · NI £1,274.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £24,079.27 a year (£2,006.61 a month)
  • Rest of UK with the same pension: take-home £24,039.60

The pension costs £1,075.26 of Scottish take-home for £1,500.00 saved. Each £1 sacrificed saves 21p tax and 8p NI here.

With a Plan 4 student loan

Most Scottish graduates who studied in Scotland repay Plan 4: 9% above £33,795. At £30,000 nothing is repaid, because pay is below the threshold. See Plan 4 in Scotland.

Other Scottish salaries: £28,000 · £35,000 · £40,000 · £45,000 · £50,000 · £55,000 · £30,000 in the rest of the UK

Curious how this changes with a 5% pension contribution?

A 5% salary-sacrifice pension on £30,000 in Scotland puts £1,500.00 a year into your pension but cuts take-home by only £1,075.26, to £24,079.27 (£2,006.61 a month). Each £1 saved costs you about 72p. See our salary sacrifice guide for how that compares with a net pay scheme.

Moving south of the border?

The same £30,000 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland leaves £25,119.60 a year (£2,093.30 a month): £34.93 less than in Scotland, because only income tax changes and NI is identical. Compare with £30,000 in the rest of the UK.

Your monthly payslip at £30,000 in Scotland

With tax code S1257L and monthly pay, a £30,000 salary shows roughly: gross £2,500.00, Scottish income tax £287.59, NI £116.20, take-home £2,096.21. If your code starts with 1257L and no S, HMRC thinks you live outside Scotland; tell HMRC if your main home is in Scotland, or your tax will be wrong.

Edge cases at this salary

  • A £1,000 rise keeps £710.00 in Scotland (£720.00 elsewhere in the UK).
  • Relief-at-source pensions. Your provider adds 20% relief. At this salary your top rate is 21%, so you can claim the extra 1% on contributions that come out of 21% income, through Self Assessment or by contacting HMRC. For £1,000 of gross contributions that is up to £10.00 a year.
  • Plan 4 student loan. Nothing is repaid below £33,795. Plan 4 is written off 30 years after you become eligible to repay, or at age 65 for older loans.
  • Moving. If you move to or from Scotland during the tax year, Scottish rates apply for the whole year if you lived in Scotland for longer than anywhere else in the UK.

More questions

Does Scottish tax affect my NI or student loan? No. NI and loan repayments are UK-wide and are based on gross pay, so only the income tax line differs.

Are savings interest and dividends taxed at Scottish rates? No. They use UK rates and allowances, even for Scottish taxpayers.
