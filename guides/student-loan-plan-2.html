GUIDE

Plan 2 on £35,000

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · One loan plan

Scope: Plan 2 only (England and Wales courses started September 2012 to July 2023).

The calculator models one student loan plan at a time. A real payslip can show more than one. The threshold and rate below are the 2026/27 HMRC collection figures used on this site.

Who Plan 2 is for

Plan 2 is the undergraduate plan for most English and Welsh courses that started from September 2012, up to the point a newer English loan is issued under Plan 5. It is not the Scottish undergraduate plan (that is Plan 4, threshold £33,795) and it is not the postgraduate loan (6% above £21,000). The threshold in this model is £29,385 at 9%. The worked £35,000 example below is unchanged so it still lines up with the Plan 5 comparison further down.

Worked example

Salary £35,000.00. Plan 2 threshold £29,385. Rate 9% of earnings above the threshold.

  • Income tax: £4,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £1,794.40
  • Plan 2: 9% × (£35,000 − £29,385) = £505.35
  • Illustrated take-home: £28,214.25 a year (£2,351.19 a month)

Without the loan, the same salary illustrates as £28,719.60. The loan is the difference in this model.

Plan 2 rules worth knowing

  • Monthly threshold. Repayments are taken each pay period above £2,448 a month (£565 a week). A low month repays nothing, even if the yearly salary is above £29,385.
  • Interest. Interest is linked to RPI, plus up to 3% depending on income, subject to government caps. Higher earners are charged more.
  • Write-off. Any balance is cancelled 30 years after the April you were first due to repay.
  • Overpaying. If your income for the whole year was below the threshold but deductions were taken in some months, you can ask the Student Loans Company for a refund.
  • Self-employed. Repayments are worked out through Self Assessment.

Should I overpay Plan 2?

Many Plan 2 borrowers never clear the balance before write-off, so voluntary overpayments can be money they would never have had to pay. Whether it helps depends on your expected lifetime earnings. Source: GOV.UK repaying your student loan.

Quick answers

When do I start repaying Plan 2? When pay is above £29,385 a year (£2,448.75 a month). Employers deduct it through PAYE each pay period.

How much is Plan 2 on £39,385? 9% × £10,000 = £900.00 a year, £75.00 a month.

Plan 2 versus Plan 5 on the same £35,000

Both plans repay 9% above a threshold. Plan 2’s 2026/27 threshold here is £29,385; Plan 5’s is £25,000. On £35,000 with no pension:

  • Plan 2 deduction: £505.35 → take-home £28,214.25
  • Plan 5 deduction: £900.00 → take-home £27,819.60 (see the Plan 5 guide)
  • Difference in this model: £394.65 a year more deducted under Plan 5 at this salary

Which plan applies is decided by the Student Loans Company and your loan start date, not by this page.

Second example — Plan 2 on £45,000, month by month

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £45,000 gross, paid in 12 equal monthly amounts · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · no pension · Plan 2 only · NI modelled on the year.

At £45,000 the salary is still inside the 20% band, so the extra £10,000 over the first example is all 20% tax, 8% NI and 9% Plan 2.

  • Income tax: 20% × £32,430 = £6,486.00 (£540.50 a month)
  • Employee NI: 8% × £32,430 = £2,594.40 (£216.20 a month)
  • Plan 2: 9% × (£45,000 − £29,385) = 9% × £15,615 = £1,405.35 (£117.11 a month)
  • Illustrated take-home: £34,514.25 a year (£2,876.19 a month)

Compared with Plan 1 on the same £45,000 (threshold £26,900): Plan 1 takes 9% × £18,100 = £1,629.00 (£135.75 a month), leaving £34,290.60. Plan 2 leaves £223.65 a year more, about £18.64 a month, because its threshold is £2,485 higher. Plan 5 would take £1,800.00 (£150.00 a month).

What this means: every extra £1,000 of salary above the threshold adds £90 a year (£7.50 a month) to Plan 2 repayments. Going from £35,000 to £45,000 adds £900 a year to the deduction. Try your own salary and plan in the calculator, or compare with the Plan 1 guide.

What this page does not decide

It does not say which plan a person is on. The Student Loans Company and the payslip do. It does not model two plans together.

Sources (checked 14 October 2026): HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
