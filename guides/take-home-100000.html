GUIDE

Take-home pay around £100,000

Last checked 30 September 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

At £100,000 the standard personal allowance is still £12,570. Above £100,000 it falls by £1 for every £2 of income. It is gone at £125,140. That taper is why a rise through this band keeps less than the 40% headline suggests.

Worked cases

£100,000

  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Income tax: £27,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £4,010.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £68,557.40 a year (£5,713.12 a month)

£110,000

  • Personal allowance in this run: £7,570.00
  • Income tax: £33,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £4,210.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £72,357.40 a year (£6,029.78 a month)

The extra £10,000 from £100,000 to £110,000 becomes £3,800.00 of take-home in this model. Tax takes £6,000.00. National Insurance takes £200.00. Part of the tax is the allowance being withdrawn, not only the 40% rate.

What this page does not decide

Pension contributions, gift aid and a non-standard tax code can change the taper. This page does not tell anyone to pay more into a pension.

Sources: HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
