Take-home pay on £100,000

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £100,000.00 offer letter is not a £100,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £100,000.00 gross

A salary of £100,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £100,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £100,000.00 − £12,570.00 = £87,430.00
  • Income tax: £27,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £4,010.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £68,557.40 a year (£5,713.12 a month · £1,318.41 a week)

You keep £68,557.40 of £100,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £100,000 is where the personal-allowance taper begins in this model (£1 of allowance lost for every £2 of income above £100,000). Higher-rate tax and upper-rate NI already apply on large slices of pay. Small gross rises near this hinge can move take-home by more than the headline suggests — run the calculator with nearby figures rather than interpolating by eye.

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £68,557.40 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay and the student-loan guides for how those toggles change a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise or nearby round number — use the neighbouring salary pages and what a £5,000 pay rise is worth.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £100,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £100k angle: the 62% trap

Between £100,000 and £125,140, every £2 of income removes £1 of personal allowance. That makes the marginal income tax rate 60%, plus 2% NI: 62p of every extra pound. At £110,000, sacrificing £10,000 into a pension brings pay back to £100,000 and restores the full allowance. Take-home falls from £72,357.40 to £68,557.40, only £3,800.00 for £10,000 in the pension.

Quick answers

What is £100,000 a month after tax? £5,713.12 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £5,438.11 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £100,000? About £51.28 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £35.16 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £380.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £100,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £68,557.40 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
