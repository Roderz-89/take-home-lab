Take-home pay on £28,000 — Scotland

Last checked 3 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

At £28,000 Scottish income tax is lower than the rest of the UK in this model, not the same and not higher. The salary sits entirely inside the Scottish starter and basic bands. It never reaches the 21% intermediate band, which starts at £29,527. Employee National Insurance is the UK charge either way, so it cancels out of the comparison.

Scotland

  • Gross: £28,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570.00 · taxable £15,430.00
  • Starter 19% on £3,967 (£12,571 to £16,537): £753.73
  • Scottish basic 20% on the remaining £11,463 (up to £28,000, still under £29,526): £2,292.60
  • Scottish income tax: £3,046.33
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × £15,430 = £1,234.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £23,719.27 a year (£1,976.61 a month)

Same salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland

  • Income tax: 20% × £15,430 = £3,086.00 (one band, no 19% starter)
  • Employee NI: £1,234.40 — identical
  • Illustrated take-home: £23,679.60 a year (£1,973.30 a month)

The gap

Income tax in Scotland minus the rest of the UK: £3,046.33 − £3,086.00 = −£39.67 a year. Take-home is therefore £39.67 higher in Scotland (£23,719.27 versus £23,679.60). The whole £39.67 is 1 percentage point on the £3,967 starter slice. Nothing else differs.

This is the opposite direction from the £45,000 comparison, where Scottish tax is about £396 more, because £45,000 has crossed the Scottish higher rate at £43,663. £28,000 has not crossed anything above 20%.
