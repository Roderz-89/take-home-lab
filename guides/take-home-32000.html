Take-home pay on £32,000

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 14 October 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £32,000.00 offer letter is not a £32,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £32,000.00 gross

A salary of £32,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £32,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £32,000.00 − £12,570.00 = £19,430.00
  • Income tax: £3,886.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £1,554.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £26,559.60 a year (£2,213.30 a month · £510.76 a week)

You keep £26,559.60 of £32,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £32,000 is still basic-rate and main NI in this model. Each extra pound above the personal allowance keeps 72 pence after 20% tax and 8% NI. See take-home on £50,270 for the higher-rate / upper-NI threshold.

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £26,559.60 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay and the student-loan guides for how those toggles change a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise or nearby round number — use the neighbouring salary pages and what a £5,000 pay rise is worth.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £32,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £32k angle: which student loan bites

£32,000 is above the Plan 2 threshold (£29,385) and the Plan 5 threshold (£25,000) but below Plan 4 (£33,795). Plan 2 takes £235.35 a year (£19.61 a month), Plan 5 takes £630.00 (£52.50 a month), and a Scottish Plan 4 borrower repays nothing. Compare all plans on Plan 2.

Quick answers

What is £32,000 a month after tax? £2,213.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £2,214.54 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £32,000? About £16.41 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £13.62 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £720.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £32,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £26,559.60 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
