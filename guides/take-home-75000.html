What’s £75k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £75,000.00 offer letter is not a £75,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. £75,000 often appears in senior IC and manager job ads; this page shows how much of that headline survives income tax and employee NI before pension or student loan. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £75,000.00 gross

A salary of £75,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £75,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £75,000.00 − £12,570 = £62,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £37,700 = £7,540.00, plus 40% × £24,730 = £9,892.00 → £17,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£50,270 − £12,570) + 2% × (£75,000 − £50,270) = £3,510.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £54,057.40 a year (£4,504.78 a month · £1,039.57 a week)

You keep £54,057.40 of £75,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £75,000 sits deeper into higher-rate territory. The first £37,700 of taxable pay is basic-rate 20%; the remaining £24,730 is higher-rate 40%. NI is 8% up to £50,270 and 2% on the £24,730 above that. Compare What’s £70k take-home pay? and Around £100,000 (where the personal-allowance taper begins at £100,000).

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A Plan 2 loan, for example, takes 9% of pay above its threshold; a salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £54,057.40 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay for how a 5% pension changes the packet on a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise toward £100,000 — still higher-rate until the additional-rate band, then the personal-allowance taper from £100,000. Around £100,000 works the taper at £100,000 and £110,000.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the same salary on both stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £75,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £75k angle: the Scottish advanced rate starts

£75,000 is the top of the Scottish 42% band; above it the Scottish advanced rate is 45%. At exactly £75,000, Scottish income tax is £19,482.05 against £17,432.00 here, so a Scottish taxpayer keeps £2,050.05 less (£52,007.35 a year). Compare Scotland versus the rest of the UK.

Quick answers

What is £75,000 a month after tax? £4,504.78 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £4,333.95 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £75,000? About £38.46 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £27.72 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £580.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £75,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £54,057.40 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
