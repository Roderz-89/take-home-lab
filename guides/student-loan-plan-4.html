GUIDE

Plan 4 on £42,000 in Scotland

Last checked 3 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland · No pension · Plan 4 only

Plan 4 is the Scottish undergraduate student loan. In this model the 2026/27 threshold is £33,795 and the rate is 9% above it — a higher threshold than Plan 1 (£26,900), Plan 2 (£29,385) or Plan 5 (£25,000). Because the borrower in this example lives in Scotland, income tax uses the Scottish bands. Employee National Insurance stays on the UK scale. A Plan 4 deduction on an English tax code would be a different page; this one does not do that.

Worked example — £42,000, Scottish tax, Plan 4

Ewan earns £42,000 in Scotland. No pension. Personal allowance £12,570, so taxable pay is £29,430. That reaches the Scottish intermediate band (21%, which runs to £43,662) but not the Scottish higher rate at £43,663. Plan 4 only sees the £8,205 above £33,795.

  • Scottish income tax: £5,971.07 — starter 19% £753.73 on £3,967, basic 20% £2,597.80 on £12,989, intermediate 21% £2,619.54 on £12,474
  • Employee National Insurance (UK): 8% × (£42,000 − £12,570) = £2,354.40
  • Plan 4: 9% × (£42,000 − £33,795) = 9% × £8,205 = £738.45
  • Illustrated take-home: £32,936.08 a year (£2,744.67 a month)

Without the loan the same Scottish salary illustrates as £33,674.53. The loan, not the tax bands, accounts for the £738.45 gap. On an English tax code the income tax at £42,000 would be lower (still all at 20%), but this page keeps Scotland on so the illustration matches a Plan 4 borrower working in Scotland.

At £32,000, the salary used on the Plan 1 guide, Plan 4 would deduct £0 because £32,000 is under £33,795. Plan 1 at that salary deducts £459.

What this page does not decide

It does not assign you to Plan 4. The Student Loans Company does. It does not model a postgraduate loan on top, and it does not give a Scottish tax residence opinion.

Sources: HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027; Scottish income tax bands in the site engine. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
