What’s £80k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

£80,000 puts £29,730 of pay in the 40% band. It is also exactly where the High Income Child Benefit Charge claws back 100% of Child Benefit. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £80,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £80,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £67,430
  • Basic 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00
  • Higher 40% on £29,730: £11,892.00
  • Income tax: £19,432.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × (£80,000 − £50,270) = £3,610.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £56,957.40 a year (£4,746.45 a month · £1,095.33 a week)

You keep 71.2% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 58p (40% tax and 2% NI).

Second example — £80,000 with a 10% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £80,000 gross · 10% salary-sacrifice pension (£8,000.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £16,232.00 · employee NI: £3,450.60
  • Pension contribution: £8,000.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £52,317.40 a year (£4,359.78 a month)

Take-home falls by £4,640.00 while £8,000.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £3,360.00. Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £80k angle: Child Benefit fully clawed back, and Scotland at 45%

At £80,000 of adjusted net income the Child Benefit charge equals the whole benefit, so a claiming parent gets nothing net. In Scotland, £5,000 of this salary sits in the 45% advanced band (above £75,000); Scottish income tax is £21,732.05 against £19,432.00, so Scottish take-home is £2,300.05 lower (£54,657.35). See Scotland versus the rest of the UK.

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.
