GUIDE

From £15 an hour to a packet

Last checked 2 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

An hourly rate is not a salary until the hours are stated. This page uses 37.5 hours a week and 52 paid weeks. A contract with fewer hours, unpaid weeks or overtime will not match.

Worked example — 37.5 hours × 52 weeks

  • £15 × 37.5 × 52 = £29,250.00 of gross in this model
  • Income tax: £3,336.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £1,334.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £24,579.60 a year (£2,048.30 a month)

That sits close to the £28,000 and £30,000 salary landings once you change hours or weeks.

Run £29,250.00 in the calculator

Same rate, fewer hours

If the contract is 30 hours a week at £15 for 52 paid weeks, gross is £23,400. With the same assumptions (England, no pension, no loan):

  • Taxable pay after £12,570: £10,830 → income tax £2,166.00
  • Employee NI on £10,830: £866.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £20,367.60 a year (£1,697.30 a month)

The hourly rate did not change. The packet did, because the hours did.

How to turn your rate into a figure here

  1. Write down contracted hours per week and paid weeks per year (not every rota pays 52).
  2. Multiply rate × hours × weeks to get annual gross.
  3. Paste that gross into the calculator, then toggle pension and student loan to match your payslip.

What this page does not decide

It does not convert a rota. Holiday pay, overtime premia, unpaid leave, zero-hours averages and a tax code are outside the illustration. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
