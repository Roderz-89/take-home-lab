What’s £65k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

£65,000 is well into the higher-rate band. £14,730 of pay is above £50,270, taxed at 40% with 2% NI. It is also inside the High Income Child Benefit Charge range, which the calculator does not model. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £65,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £65,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £52,430
  • Basic 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00
  • Higher 40% on £14,730: £5,892.00
  • Income tax: £13,432.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × (£65,000 − £50,270) = £3,310.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £48,257.40 a year (£4,021.45 a month · £928.03 a week)

You keep 74.2% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 58p (40% tax and 2% NI).

Second example — £65,000 with a 10% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £65,000 gross · 10% salary-sacrifice pension (£6,500.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £10,832.00 · employee NI: £3,180.60
  • Pension contribution: £6,500.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £44,487.40 a year (£3,707.28 a month)

Take-home falls by £3,770.00 while £6,500.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £2,730.00. Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £65k angle: a quarter of Child Benefit clawed back

The High Income Child Benefit Charge is 1% of the Child Benefit received for every £200 of adjusted net income above £60,000. At £65,000 that is 25%. For a family claiming for one child (£27.05 a week in 2026/27, about £1,406.60 a year) the charge would be about £351.65, paid through Self Assessment. Sacrificing £5,000 into a pension brings adjusted net income to £60,000 and removes the charge as well as saving 42% on that pay. Details: GOV.UK High Income Child Benefit Charge.

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.
