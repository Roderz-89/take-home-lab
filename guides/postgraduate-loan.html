Postgraduate loan on £40,000

Last checked 3 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · Postgraduate loan only

A postgraduate loan (master’s, and in some cases a doctoral loan collected the same way) is not a 9% undergraduate plan. In this model the 2026/27 threshold is £21,000 and the rate is 6% of earnings above it. The lower threshold does not mean a larger deduction than Plan 5 at every salary, because 6% is a third smaller than 9%.

Many people repay a postgraduate loan and an undergraduate plan in the same month. This calculator still models one plan. The figures below are the postgraduate line on its own.

Worked example — £40,000 in England

Amira earns £40,000, standard allowance, no pension, postgraduate loan only. Taxable pay £27,430 is still inside the 20% band (the rest-of-UK higher rate starts at £50,270).

  • Income tax: 20% × £27,430 = £5,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£40,000 − £12,570) = £2,194.40
  • Postgraduate loan: 6% × (£40,000 − £21,000) = 6% × £19,000 = £1,140.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £31,179.60 a year (£2,598.30 a month)

Without the loan, take-home illustrates as £32,319.60. If the same £19,000 slice were charged at the undergraduate 9% rate, the deduction would be £1,710, not £1,140. The 6% rate is why a postgraduate loan on £40,000 (£1,140) is larger than Plan 2 on £35,000 (£505.35) but not twice as large as the extra earnings might suggest.

For a sense of the undergraduate plan alone at this salary, Plan 2 on £40,000 with no pension deducts £955.35 (9% of £40,000 − £29,385) and take-home illustrates as £31,364.25. Adding that to the postgraduate figure would be a second deduction this page refuses to invent as a combined payslip.

What this page does not decide
