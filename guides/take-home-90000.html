What’s £90k take-home pay?

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 17 October 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 17 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

£90,000 is the last round salary before the personal allowance taper. All £39,730 above £50,270 is taxed at 40% with 2% NI, and the full £12,570 allowance still applies. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £90,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £90,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £77,430
  • Basic 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00
  • Higher 40% on £39,730: £15,892.00
  • Income tax: £23,432.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × (£90,000 − £50,270) = £3,810.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £62,757.40 a year (£5,229.78 a month · £1,206.87 a week)

You keep 69.7% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 58p (40% tax and 2% NI).

Curious how this changes with a 5% pension contribution?

A 5% salary-sacrifice pension on £90,000 puts £4,500.00 a year into your pension but cuts take-home by only £2,610.00, to £60,147.40 (£5,012.28 a month). Each £1 saved costs you about 58p. See our salary sacrifice guide for how that compares with a net pay scheme.

Moving north of the border?

On Scottish income tax bands, £90,000 leaves £59,957.35 a year (£4,996.45 a month): £2,800.05 less than here. NI does not change. Compare with the nearest Scottish worked example, £55,000 in Scotland, or Scotland versus the rest of the UK.

Second example — £90,000 with a 10% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £90,000 gross · 10% salary-sacrifice pension (£9,000.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £19,832.00 · employee NI: £3,630.60
  • Pension contribution: £9,000.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £57,537.40 a year (£4,794.78 a month)

Take-home falls by £5,220.00 while £9,000.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £3,780.00. Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £90k angle: £10,000 from the taper

A rise from £90,000 to £100,000 adds £5,800.00 to take-home (58p a pound). The next £10,000, to £110,000, adds only £3,800.00, because £5,000 of personal allowance is withdrawn: a 62% marginal deduction. If a pay rise or bonus will cross £100,000, see £100,000 and the taper.

Nearby salaries: £80,000 · £100,000 · all salaries on the guides hub.

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. A company car, van or health insurance is added to your taxable income, so at £90,000 a benefit worth more than £10,000 would take you into the £100,000 personal allowance taper. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.

Planning around £100,000

  • Bonuses. A £15,000 bonus on £90,000 adds £7,700.00: £5,800.00 on the first £10,000, but only £1,900.00 on the last £5,000, which is in the taper.
  • Childcare. If either parent’s adjusted net income goes above £100,000, the family loses Tax-Free Childcare (up to £2,000 a child a year) and the extra free childcare hours in England.
  • Pension. Sacrificing the part of a bonus that goes over £100,000 keeps the full personal allowance and saves 62% on that slice.

Quick answers

What is £90,000 a month after tax? £5,229.78 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £4,996.45 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £90,000? About £46.15 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £32.18 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £580.00 at this salary.
