What’s £90k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

£90,000 is the last round salary before the personal allowance taper. All £39,730 above £50,270 is taxed at 40% with 2% NI, and the full £12,570 allowance still applies. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £90,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £90,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £77,430
  • Basic 20% on £37,700: £7,540.00
  • Higher 40% on £39,730: £15,892.00
  • Income tax: £23,432.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × (£90,000 − £50,270) = £3,810.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £62,757.40 a year (£5,229.78 a month · £1,206.87 a week)

You keep 69.7% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 58p (40% tax and 2% NI).

Second example — £90,000 with a 10% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £90,000 gross · 10% salary-sacrifice pension (£9,000.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £19,832.00 · employee NI: £3,630.60
  • Pension contribution: £9,000.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £57,537.40 a year (£4,794.78 a month)

Take-home falls by £5,220.00 while £9,000.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £3,780.00. Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £90k angle: £10,000 from the taper

A rise from £90,000 to £100,000 adds £5,800.00 to take-home (58p a pound). The next £10,000, to £110,000, adds only £3,800.00, because £5,000 of personal allowance is withdrawn: a 62% marginal deduction. If a pay rise or bonus will cross £100,000, see £100,000 and the taper.

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.
