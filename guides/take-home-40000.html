£40,000 after tax in the UK (2026/27)

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 12 November 2026 · Editorial policy

On a £40,000 salary you take home £32,319.60 a year — £2,693.30 a month or £621.53 a week — in England, Wales or Northern Ireland for 2026/27, with no pension and no student loan.

Updated 12 November 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

How much tax on a £40k salary? £5,486.00 income tax plus £2,194.40 employee National Insurance, so £7,680.40 comes out of the £40,000 before it reaches your account. All of it is basic-rate tax and main-rate NI. The worked lines are below.

Worked example — £40,000 gross

A salary of £40,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £40,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £40,000 − £12,570 = £27,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £27,430 = £5,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£40,000 − £12,570) = £2,194.40
  • Take-home: £32,319.60 a year (£2,693.30 a month · £621.53 a week)

You keep £32,319.60 of £40,000.00.

Moving north of the border?

On Scottish income tax bands, £40,000 leaves £32,254.53 a year (£2,687.88 a month): £65.07 less than here. NI does not change. Compare with the Scotland column of the take-home pay table.

The £40k angle: Plan 2 versus Plan 5

The same £40,000 salary repays very different amounts depending on when the course started. Plan 2 takes £955.35 a year (take-home £31,364.25), Plan 5 takes £1,350.00 (take-home £30,969.60), a gap of £394.65. A postgraduate loan alone takes £1,140.00. See Plan 5 and postgraduate loan.

Checking a £40,000 monthly payslip

On a standard 1257L code, paid monthly, a £40,000 payslip should show roughly £3,333.33 gross, £457.17 income tax, £182.87 National Insurance and £2,693.30 net. If your tax line is far off, look at the tax code first; if NI is off, look for a pension or other salary sacrifice.

Loan plan makes a real difference here: Plan 2 takes £955.35 a year and Plan 5 takes £1,350, so a Plan 5 graduate keeps £394.65 a year less than a Plan 2 graduate on the same salary.

Quick answers

What is £40,000 a month after tax? £2,693.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £2,687.88 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £40,000? About £20.51 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £16.57 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.
