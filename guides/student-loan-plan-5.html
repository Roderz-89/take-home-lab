Plan 5 on £28,000 — just over the threshold

Last checked 3 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · Plan 5 only

Plan 5 is the newer English undergraduate loan. On this site it applies to new undergraduate loans issued from 6 April 2026 in England. The 2026/27 collection threshold in the calculator is £25,000 — the lowest undergraduate threshold modelled here — at 9%. Plan 2, still common for English and Welsh courses that started from 2012, uses £29,385. That £4,385 gap between the two thresholds is the whole point of this example.

Worked example — £28,000 in England

Noah earns £28,000, standard allowance, no pension. Taxable pay is £15,430, all at basic rate. The salary is above Plan 5 and below Plan 2.

  • Income tax: 20% × (£28,000 − £12,570) = £3,086.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × £15,430 = £1,234.40
  • Plan 5: 9% × (£28,000 − £25,000) = 9% × £3,000 = £270.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £23,409.60 a year (£1,950.80 a month)

Without any loan the same salary illustrates as £23,679.60. Switch the calculator to Plan 2 at this exact salary and the loan line is £0.00, because £28,000 is under the £29,385 Plan 2 threshold. Plan 5 is the only undergraduate plan in this tool that takes a deduction at £28,000 (Plan 1 would too, at a different amount: 9% of £1,100). Plan 4 would not.

Why the Plan 2 page still quotes £35,000

At £35,000, Plan 5 deducts 9% × £10,000 = £900.00 and take-home illustrates as £27,819.60. Plan 2 on that same salary deducts £505.35. The Plan 2 guide keeps that side-by-side. This page uses £28,000 so the story is the threshold itself, not another copy of the £35,000 stack.

Run £28,000 with Plan 5

Second example — Plan 5 on £45,000, month by month

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £45,000 gross, paid in 12 equal monthly amounts · England · no pension · Plan 5 only · NI modelled on the year.

At £45,000 the threshold gap between Plan 5 and Plan 2 still matters, but it is now a fixed amount rather than the whole deduction.

  • Income tax: 20% × £32,430 = £6,486.00 (£540.50 a month)
  • Employee NI: 8% × £32,430 = £2,594.40 (£216.20 a month)
  • Plan 5: 9% × (£45,000 − £25,000) = 9% × £20,000 = £1,800.00 (£150.00 a month)
  • Illustrated take-home: £34,119.60 a year (£2,843.30 a month)

Compared with Plan 2 on the same £45,000: Plan 2 takes £1,405.35 (£117.11 a month), leaving £34,514.25. Plan 5 takes £394.65 a year more, about £32.89 a month. That is 9% of the £4,385 threshold gap, the same £394.65 as at £35,000. Once you earn above both thresholds the difference stays the same.

What this means: on a salary above £29,385, Plan 5 costs about £33 a month more than Plan 2 in this model, whatever the salary. Below that, only Plan 5 takes anything. Try your own figures in the calculator, or read the Plan 2 guide.

What this page does not decide

Course start date and the Student Loans Company decide the plan. This page does not.

Sources: HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027. Figures from js/tax.js. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
