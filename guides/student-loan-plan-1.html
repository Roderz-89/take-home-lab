Plan 1 on £32,000

Last checked 3 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · Plan 1 only

Plan 1 is the older undergraduate repayment plan still collected through PAYE for many people who studied in England or Wales before the 2012 funding change, and for Northern Ireland undergraduate loans. This site’s 2026/27 collection threshold for Plan 1 is £26,900, and the rate is 9% of earnings above that line. It is not the plan for a new English undergraduate loan, and it is not the Scottish Plan 4.

The calculator models one plan at a time. A payslip can show an undergraduate plan and a postgraduate loan together. This page does not add them.

Worked example — £32,000 in England

Priya earns £32,000, with a standard personal allowance, no pension in the model, and Plan 1 only. Taxable pay is £19,430, all inside the 20% basic-rate band. Employee National Insurance is 8% of the slice between £12,570 and £32,000.

  • Income tax: 20% × £19,430 = £3,886.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × £19,430 = £1,554.40
  • Plan 1: 9% × (£32,000 − £26,900) = 9% × £5,100 = £459.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £26,100.60 a year (£2,175.05 a month)

Turn the loan off and the same salary illustrates as £26,559.60. The £459 is the whole difference. £32,000 is used here on purpose: it is only £5,100 over the Plan 1 threshold, so the deduction is smaller than the Plan 2 example on £35,000 even though Plan 1’s threshold is lower. Someone on Plan 4 (threshold £33,795) would pay nothing at £32,000, because the salary has not cleared that line.

What this page does not decide

It does not tell you which plan the Student Loans Company has you on. Check the payslip code or your SLC account. Interest on the loan balance is not modelled — only the PAYE deduction.

Sources: HMRC rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027. Figures from the Take-Home Lab engine in js/tax.js. National Insurance is modelled on the year, not each pay period.
