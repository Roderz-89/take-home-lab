Take-home pay on £28,000

Last checked 2 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

A £28,000.00 offer letter is not a £28,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £28,000.00 gross

A salary of £28,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £28,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £28,000.00 − £12,570.00 = £15,430.00
  • Income tax: £3,086.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £1,234.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £23,679.60 a year (£1,973.30 a month · £455.38 a week)

You keep £23,679.60 of £28,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £28,000 sits inside the rest-of-UK basic-rate band and the NI main rate. All of the taxable slice is taxed at 20%, and all of the NI band is at the main 8% rate. There is no higher-rate tax and no 2% NI upper rate — those appear once pay crosses £50,270.

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £23,679.60 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay and the student-loan guides for how those toggles change a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise or nearby round number — use the neighbouring salary pages and what a £5,000 pay rise is worth.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £28,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £28,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £23,679.60 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
