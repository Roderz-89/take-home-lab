Take-home pay on £28,000

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 12 November 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 12 November 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see £28,000 in Scotland; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

On a £28,000 salary you take home £23,679.60 a year — £1,973.30 a month or £455.38 a week — in England, Wales or Northern Ireland for 2026/27, with no pension and no student loan. That is after £3,086.00 income tax and £1,234.40 National Insurance. At this salary which student loan plan you are on decides whether you repay nothing or £270 a year.

Worked example — £28,000.00 gross

A salary of £28,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £28,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £28,000.00 − £12,570.00 = £15,430.00
  • Income tax: £3,086.00
  • Employee National Insurance: £1,234.40

You keep £23,679.60 of £28,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

Moving north of the border?

On Scottish income tax bands, £28,000 leaves £23,719.27 a year (£1,976.61 a month): £39.67 more than here. NI does not change. Compare with £28,000 in Scotland.

The £28k angle: Scotland is cheaper here

At £28,000 the Scottish starter band (19%) saves money and the 21% intermediate band has not started. Scottish income tax is £3,046.33 against £3,086.00 here, so a Scottish taxpayer keeps £39.67 more (£23,719.27 a year). Worked line by line on £28,000 in Scotland.

Which loan plan takes what at £28,000

  • Plan 1 (threshold £26,900): £99 a year.
  • Plan 2 (£29,385) and Plan 4 (£33,795): nothing yet.
  • Plan 5 (£25,000): £270 a year.
  • Postgraduate Loan (6% above £21,000): £420 a year.

If your payslip shows a deduction you did not expect, check the plan type on it first: an employer using the wrong plan is a common reason for a loan deduction to be off by hundreds of pounds a year. A Scottish taxpayer on £28,000 pays £39.67 less income tax, because the 19% starter band outweighs anything in the 21% band.
