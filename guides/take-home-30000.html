What’s £30k take-home pay?

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 12 November 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 12 November 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see £30,000 in Scotland; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

On a £30,000 salary you take home £25,119.60 a year — £2,093.30 a month or £483.07 a week — in England, Wales or Northern Ireland for 2026/27, with no pension and no student loan. That is after £3,486.00 income tax and £1,394.40 National Insurance. £30,000 is the first round number where a Plan 2 loan takes something, and where pension choices start to show on the payslip.

Worked example — £30,000.00 gross

A salary of £30,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £30,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £30,000.00 − £12,570 = £17,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £17,430 = £3,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£30,000 − £12,570) = £1,394.40
  • Take-home: £25,119.60 a year (£2,093.30 a month · £483.07 a week)

You keep £25,119.60 of £30,000.00.

Moving north of the border?

On Scottish income tax bands, £30,000 leaves £25,154.53 a year (£2,096.21 a month): £34.93 more than here. NI does not change. Compare with £30,000 in Scotland.

The £30k angle: what a 5% salary-sacrifice pension really costs

A 5% salary sacrifice at £30,000 puts £1,500.00 a year into the pension. Take-home falls from £25,119.60 to £24,039.60, only £1,080.00 less, because the sacrificed pay escapes 20% tax and 8% NI. Each £1 in the pension costs 72p of take-home. More on salary sacrifice versus net pay.

Net pay or salary sacrifice at £30,000

A 5% pension (£1,500 a year) costs different amounts depending on how your employer runs it. Through a net pay scheme it cuts take-home by £1,200, because only income tax is saved. Through salary sacrifice it cuts take-home by £1,080, because NI is saved too. Same pension, £120 a year apart.

£30,000 is also the first round salary where Plan 2 takes something: £55.35 a year, about £4.61 a month. Plan 5 takes £450.

Quick answers

What is £30,000 a month after tax? £2,093.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £2,096.21 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £30,000? About £15.38 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £12.88 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £720.00 at this salary.
