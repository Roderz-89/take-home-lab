What’s £45k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see £45,000 in Scotland; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £45,000.00 offer letter is not a £45,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. £45,000 is also the gross used on the Scotland-versus-rest-of-UK guide, so this page locks the rest-of-UK side of that comparison as a standalone packet illustration. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £45,000.00 gross

A salary of £45,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £45,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £45,000.00 − £12,570 = £32,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £32,430 = £6,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£45,000 − £12,570) = £2,594.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £35,919.60 a year (£2,993.30 a month · £690.76 a week)

You keep £35,919.60 of £45,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £45,000 still sits inside the rest-of-UK basic-rate band and the NI main rate — the higher-rate / NI upper-earnings cliff is at £50,270. All taxable pay is 20%; all NI on the band is 8%. How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A Plan 2 loan, for example, takes 9% of pay above its threshold; a salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £35,919.60 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay for how a 5% pension changes the packet on a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise past £50,270 — the next pounds meet higher-rate tax and the lower NI upper rate. What a £5,000 pay rise is worth shows the basic-rate maths before that cliff; What’s £50k take-home pay? and take-home on £50,270 sit on the approach.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the same salary on both stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £45,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £45k angle: £5,270 of basic-rate headroom

At £45,000 the next £5,270 of pay is still taxed at 20% with 8% NI. A rise to £50,270 lifts take-home from £35,919.60 to £39,714.00: £3,794.40 of the £5,270.00, 72p a pound. In Scotland, £45,000 is already £1,338 into the 42% band, and take-home is £35,523.55, £396.05 less. See £45,000 in Scotland.

Quick answers

What is £45,000 a month after tax? £2,993.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £2,960.30 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £45,000? About £23.08 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £18.42 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £720.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £45,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £35,919.60 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
