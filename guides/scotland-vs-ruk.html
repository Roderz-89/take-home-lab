Scotland versus the rest of the UK on the same salary

Last checked September 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · £45,000 · No pension · No student loan

National Insurance is a UK employee charge. Income tax on wages is not. Scotland sets six bands. England, Wales and Northern Ireland use three main rates. A headline salary can therefore produce two take-home figures before anything else changes.

Worked example — £45,000

Sam has a standard personal allowance of £12,570 and no other deductions in the model.

Rest of the UK

Taxable pay £32,430, all inside the basic-rate band.

  • Income tax at 20% = £6,486.00
  • Employee NI 8% × (£45,000 − £12,570) = £2,594.40
  • Take-home: £35,919.60

Scotland

The same taxable pay is sliced across starter 19%, basic 20%, intermediate 21% and a slice of higher 42% — because £45,000 sits above the Scottish higher-rate threshold at £43,662.

  • Starter: 19% × £3,967 (£12,571 to £16,537) = £753.73
  • Basic: 20% × £12,989 (£16,538 to £29,526) = £2,597.80
  • Intermediate: 21% × £14,136 (£29,527 to £43,662) = £2,968.56
  • Higher: 42% × £1,338 (£43,663 to £45,000) = £561.96
  • Income tax total = £6,882.05
  • Employee NI is the same £2,594.40
  • Take-home = £35,523.55

On this salary the Scottish income-tax stack costs about £396 more in the year (£6,882.05 versus £6,486.00, a difference of £396.05). Take-home is £35,523.55 in Scotland and £35,919.60 in the rest of the UK. The gap appears because £45,000 has already crossed the Scottish higher rate at £43,663 while the rest of the UK is still at 20% until £50,270. Do not treat everything from £29,527 to £45,000 as intermediate — that band ends at £43,662. National Insurance is £2,594.40 in both columns, so none of the £396.05 is NI.

The gap is not the same at every salary

The three dedicated Scotland pages use this same engine. They do not copy the £45,000 gap.

  • £28,000: Scottish tax £3,046.33, rest of UK £3,086.00. Scotland is £39.67 cheaper. The salary is only in the 19% starter band and the 20% basic band. The 21% band has not started.
  • £35,000: Scottish tax £4,501.07, rest of UK £4,486.00. Scotland costs £15.07 more. The starter band saves £39.67 and the 21% intermediate band on £5,474 costs £54.74. They nearly cancel. There is still no 42% slice.
  • £55,000: Scottish tax £11,082.05, rest of UK £9,432.00. Scotland costs £1,650.05 more. £11,338 is taxed at 42% in Scotland. In the rest of the UK only £4,730 is taxed at 40%, and the rest of the taxable pay is still at 20% up to £50,270. NI is £3,110.60 on both sides.

Read the sign before you quote a single “Scotland costs more” line. Under about £30,000 in this model, Scotland can be slightly cheaper. By £35,000 it has tipped the other way by £15. By the time higher rate is open, the gap is hundreds of pounds, then well over a thousand.

What to change in the calculator

Leave pension and student loan on “none” if you want the gap above. A Plan 4 deduction or a salary sacrifice will move take-home, but it will not change the income-tax difference between the two regions unless the pension also changes taxable pay. Switching only the region dropdown replays these cases.
