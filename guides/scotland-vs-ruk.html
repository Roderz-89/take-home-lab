Scotland versus the rest of the UK on the same salary

Last checked September 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · £45,000 · No pension · No student loan

National Insurance is a UK employee charge. Income tax on wages is not. Scotland sets six bands. England, Wales and Northern Ireland use three main rates. A headline salary can therefore produce two take-home figures before anything else changes.

Worked example — £45,000

Sam has a standard personal allowance of £12,570 and no other deductions in the model.

Rest of the UK

Taxable pay £32,430, all inside the basic-rate band.

  • Income tax at 20% = £6,486.00
  • Employee NI 8% × (£45,000 − £12,570) = £2,594.40
  • Take-home: £35,919.60

Scotland

The same taxable pay is sliced across starter 19%, basic 20% and intermediate 21%.

  • Starter: 19% × £3,967 (£12,571 to £16,537) = £753.73
  • Basic: 20% × £12,989 (£16,538 to £29,526) = £2,597.80
  • Intermediate: 21% × £15,474 (£29,527 to £45,000) = £3,249.54
  • Income tax total ≈ £6,601.07
  • Employee NI is the same £2,594.40
  • Take-home ≈ £35,804.53

On this salary the Scottish income-tax stack costs about £115 more in the year. The gap is small here because £45,000 is only just into the Scottish intermediate band. It widens once pay crosses the Scottish higher rate at £43,663 while the rest of the UK is still at 20% until £50,270.
