What’s £55k take-home pay?

Last checked 1 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

A £55,000.00 offer letter is not a £55,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. At £55,000 both higher-rate income tax and the 2% NI upper rate already apply — so the packet gap versus a £50k offer is smaller than the £5,000 gross difference suggests. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £55,000.00 gross

A salary of £55,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £55,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £55,000.00 − £12,570 = £42,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £37,700 = £7,540.00, plus 40% × £4,730 = £1,892.00 → £9,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£50,270 − £12,570) + 2% × (£55,000 − £50,270) = £3,110.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £42,457.40 a year (£3,538.12 a month · £816.49 a week)

You keep £42,457.40 of £55,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £55,000 sits past the rest-of-UK higher-rate threshold and the NI upper earnings limit (both £50,270 for 2026/27). The first £37,700 of taxable pay is basic-rate 20%; the remaining £4,730 is higher-rate 40%. NI is 8% up to £50,270 and 2% on the £4,730 above that. How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A Plan 2 loan, for example, takes 9% of pay above its threshold; a salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £42,457.40 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay for how a 5% pension changes the packet on a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise further above £50,270 — each extra pound meets 40% income tax and 2% NI (42% combined) until the additional-rate band. What’s £60k take-home pay? and What’s £70k take-home pay? work the next round numbers.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the same salary on both stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £55,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £55,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £42,457.40 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
