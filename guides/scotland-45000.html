Take-home pay on £45,000 — Scotland

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

Scope: Scottish taxpayers only, one salary. For the same salary elsewhere see £45,000 in the rest of the UK.

£45,000 is the first round salary in the Scottish 42% higher band: £1,338 of pay is above £43,663, while the rest of the UK is still entirely at 20%. National Insurance is the same everywhere in the UK, so the whole difference comes from income tax. Figures are for 2026/27 and come from the same engine as the calculator.

The gap

Scottish income tax is £396.05 a year higher (about £33.00 a month). The 19% starter band saves £39.67 against the rest of the UK. The 21% intermediate band costs 1 point more than 20% on £14,136. The 42% higher band costs 22 points more than 20% on £1,338. At this salary a Scottish taxpayer’s next pound is taxed at 42%, plus 8% NI.

Second example — £45,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £45,000 gross · 5% salary sacrifice (£2,250.00) · no student loan.

  • Scottish income tax: £6,128.57 · NI £2,414.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £34,207.03 a year (£2,850.59 a month)
  • Rest of UK with the same pension: take-home £34,299.60

The pension costs £1,316.52 of Scottish take-home for £2,250.00 saved. Sacrificed pay comes first out of the 42% slice, so each £1 in the pension costs as little as 50p in Scotland, against 72p in the rest of the UK at this salary.

With a Plan 4 student loan

Most Scottish graduates who studied in Scotland repay Plan 4: 9% above £33,795. At £45,000 that is 9% × £11,205 = £1,008.45 a year (£84.04 a month), leaving £34,515.10. See Plan 4 in Scotland.
