Take-home pay on £45,000 — Scotland

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · Scotland vs England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan

Scope: Scottish taxpayers only, one salary. For the same salary elsewhere see £45,000 in the rest of the UK.

£45,000 is the first round salary in the Scottish 42% higher band: £1,338 of pay is above £43,663, while the rest of the UK is still entirely at 20%. National Insurance is the same everywhere in the UK, so the whole difference comes from income tax. Figures are for 2026/27 and come from the same engine as the calculator.

Scotland

  • Gross £45,000 · allowance £12,570 · taxable £32,430
  • Starter 19% on £3,967: £753.73
  • Basic 20% on £12,989: £2,597.80
  • Intermediate 21% on £14,136: £2,968.56
  • Higher 42% on £1,338: £561.96
  • Scottish income tax: £6,882.05
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£45,000 − £12,570) = £2,594.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £35,523.55 a year (£2,960.30 a month)

Same salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland

  • Income tax: £6,486.00 · NI £2,594.40 — identical
  • Illustrated take-home: £35,919.60 a year (£2,993.30 a month)

The gap

Scottish income tax is £396.05 a year higher (about £33.00 a month). The 19% starter band saves £39.67 against the rest of the UK. The 21% intermediate band costs 1 point more than 20% on £14,136. The 42% higher band costs 22 points more than 20% on £1,338. At this salary a Scottish taxpayer’s next pound is taxed at 42%, plus 8% NI.

Second example — £45,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £45,000 gross · 5% salary sacrifice (£2,250.00) · no student loan.

  • Scottish income tax: £6,128.57 · NI £2,414.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £34,207.03 a year (£2,850.59 a month)
  • Rest of UK with the same pension: take-home £34,299.60

The pension costs £1,316.52 of Scottish take-home for £2,250.00 saved. Sacrificed pay comes first out of the 42% slice, so each £1 in the pension costs as little as 50p in Scotland, against 72p in the rest of the UK at this salary.

With a Plan 4 student loan

Most Scottish graduates who studied in Scotland repay Plan 4: 9% above £33,795. At £45,000 that is 9% × £11,205 = £1,008.45 a year (£84.04 a month), leaving £34,515.10. See Plan 4 in Scotland.

Other Scottish salaries: £28,000 · £30,000 · £35,000 · £40,000 · £50,000 · £55,000 · £45,000 in the rest of the UK

Your monthly payslip at £45,000 in Scotland

With tax code S1257L and monthly pay, a £45,000 salary shows roughly: gross £3,750.00, Scottish income tax £573.50, NI £216.20, take-home £2,960.30. If your code starts with 1257L and no S, HMRC thinks you live outside Scotland; tell HMRC if your main home is in Scotland, or your tax will be wrong.

Edge cases at this salary

  • A £1,000 rise keeps £500.00 in Scotland (£720.00 elsewhere in the UK).
  • Relief-at-source pensions. Your provider adds 20% relief. At this salary your top rate is 42%, so you can claim the extra 22% on contributions that come out of 42% income, through Self Assessment or by contacting HMRC. For £1,000 of gross contributions that is up to £220.00 a year.
  • Plan 4 student loan. 9% above £33,795 takes £1,008.45 a year (£84.04 a month). Plan 4 is written off 30 years after you become eligible to repay, or at age 65 for older loans.
  • Moving. If you move to or from Scotland during the tax year, Scottish rates apply for the whole year if you lived in Scotland for longer than anywhere else in the UK.

More questions

Does Scottish tax affect my NI or student loan? No. NI and loan repayments are UK-wide and are based on gross pay, so only the income tax line differs.

Are savings interest and dividends taxed at Scottish rates? No. They use UK rates and allowances, even for Scottish taxpayers.
