What’s £70k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £70,000.00 offer letter is not a £70,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. £70,000 is a common mid-to-senior professional headline; once you are this far past £50,270, most of each extra pound is taxed at the combined higher-rate / upper-NI rate. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £70,000.00 gross

A salary of £70,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £70,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £70,000.00 − £12,570 = £57,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £37,700 = £7,540.00, plus 40% × £19,730 = £7,892.00 → £15,432.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£50,270 − £12,570) + 2% × (£70,000 − £50,270) = £3,410.60
  • Illustrated take-home: £51,157.40 a year (£4,263.12 a month · £983.80 a week)

You keep £51,157.40 of £70,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

EXAMPLE £70,000 sits well into higher-rate territory. The first £37,700 of taxable pay is basic-rate 20%; the remaining £19,730 is higher-rate 40%. NI is 8% up to £50,270 and 2% on the £19,730 above that. Compare What’s £60k take-home pay? and What’s £75k take-home pay? for neighbouring headlines.

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A Plan 2 loan, for example, takes 9% of pay above its threshold; a salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £51,157.40 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay for how a 5% pension changes the packet on a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise further above £50,270 — each extra pound meets 40% income tax and 2% NI (42% combined). What’s £75k take-home pay? and Around £100,000 (where the personal-allowance taper begins) sit further up the scale.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the same salary on both stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £70,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £70k angle: pension is cheap in the 40% band

A 10% salary sacrifice at £70,000 puts £7,000.00 a year into the pension. All of it comes out of 40% tax and 2% NI pay, so take-home falls by only £4,060.00, from £51,157.40 to £47,097.40. Each £1 in the pension costs 58p. Sacrificing down to £60,000 can also cut the Child Benefit charge (see £60k).

Quick answers

What is £70,000 a month after tax? £4,263.12 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £4,100.61 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £70,000? About £35.90 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £26.23 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £580.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £70,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £51,157.40 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
