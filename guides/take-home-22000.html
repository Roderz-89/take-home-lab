What’s £22k take-home pay?

By the Rodway Labs editorial team · Checked 17 October 2026 · Editorial policy

Updated 17 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see Scotland versus the rest of the UK; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

£22,000 is common for apprenticeships after year one, care, hospitality and junior admin roles. Everything above the £12,570 allowance is taxed at the 20% basic rate and 8% NI. This page works the 2026/27 figures line by line for England, Wales or Northern Ireland, using the same engine as the Take-Home Lab calculator. It is an illustration, not a payslip or tax advice.

Worked example — £22,000 gross

  • Gross pay: £22,000.00 · personal allowance £12,570 · taxable £9,430
  • Basic 20% on £9,430: £1,886.00
  • Income tax: £1,886.00
  • Employee NI: 8% × (£22,000 − £12,570) = £754.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £19,359.60 a year (£1,613.30 a month · £372.30 a week)

You keep 88.0% of the headline salary. The next pound earned keeps 72p (20% tax and 8% NI).

Curious how this changes with a 5% pension contribution?

A 5% salary-sacrifice pension on £22,000 puts £1,100.00 a year into your pension but cuts take-home by only £792.00, to £18,567.60 (£1,547.30 a month). Each £1 saved costs you about 72p. See our salary sacrifice guide for how that compares with a net pay scheme.

Moving north of the border?

On Scottish income tax bands, £22,000 leaves £19,399.27 a year (£1,616.61 a month): £39.67 more than here. NI does not change. Compare with the nearest Scottish worked example, £28,000 in Scotland, or Scotland versus the rest of the UK.

Second example — £22,000 with a 5% salary-sacrifice pension

Assumptions: 2026/27 · £22,000 gross · 5% salary-sacrifice pension (£1,100.00 a year) · standard £12,570 allowance · no student loan.

  • Income tax: £1,666.00 · employee NI: £666.40
  • Pension contribution: £1,100.00
  • Illustrated take-home: £18,567.60 a year (£1,547.30 a month)

Take-home falls by £792.00 while £1,100.00 goes into the pension: tax and NI cover the other £308.00. Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and NI. See salary sacrifice versus net pay.

The £22k angle: the postgraduate loan has started

The postgraduate loan threshold is £21,000, so at £22,000 a postgraduate borrower repays 6% × £1,000 = £60.00 a year (£5.00 a month), and take-home is £19,299.60. Undergraduate plans start higher. In Scotland, income tax is £1,846.33, so take-home is £19,399.27, £39.67 more than in the rest of the UK. See postgraduate loan.

What this page does not decide

It does not include your tax code, benefits in kind, bonuses or overtime, and it models NI on the year rather than each pay period. Check any offer against a real payslip before treating the monthly figure as a budget.

Edge cases at £22,000

  • Two jobs. If £22,000 is made up of two jobs, the personal allowance normally goes on the main job and the second job uses code BR (20% on everything). Over the year the total tax is the same, but each payslip looks different. Check your codes in your HMRC personal tax account.
  • Emergency tax. A new job without a P45 may start on an emergency code (1257L W1/M1). You may overpay for a few months; it is refunded through payroll once HMRC updates the code.
  • Marriage Allowance. If your spouse or civil partner earns under £12,570, they can transfer £1,260 of allowance to you, cutting your tax by up to £252 a year, as long as you pay basic rate. Apply on GOV.UK.
  • Universal Credit. At this pay, Universal Credit may still be payable depending on rent and children; it reduces by 55p for each £1 of take-home above any work allowance. This guide does not model it.

Weekly and four-weekly pay

Paid weekly, £22,000 is £423.08 gross and about £372.30 take-home. Paid every four weeks (13 pay days), it is £1,692.31 gross and £1,489.20 take-home a pay day.

More questions

Do I pay into a workplace pension automatically? Yes, if you earn over £10,000 a year and are aged 22 to State Pension age, you are auto-enrolled. The minimum employee contribution is usually 5% of qualifying earnings (pay above £6,240), which at £22,000 is about £788.00 a year, including tax relief.

Quick answers

What is £22,000 a month after tax? £1,613.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £1,616.61 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £22,000? About £11.28 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £9.93 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £720.00 at this salary.
