What’s £35k take-home pay?

Updated 14 October 2026 · Tax year 2026/27 · England, Wales or Northern Ireland · No pension · No student loan · Standard personal allowance

Scope: England, Wales and Northern Ireland only, one salary. For Scotland see £35,000 in Scotland; for loan detail see the student loan guides.

A £35,000.00 offer letter is not a £35,000.00 bank balance. Income tax and employee National Insurance come out first. The site’s default calculator example is also £35,000 — this page freezes that baseline as a readable guide so you can compare offers without retyping the form. This page is one worked case for the 2026/27 tax year using the same bands as the Take-Home Lab calculator — so the £ figures here match the on-page engine. It is an illustration only, not personalised tax advice or a payslip. Publisher: Rodway Labs. Contact: hello@takehomelab.co.uk.

Worked example — £35,000.00 gross

A salary of £35,000.00 with nothing else in the model: standard personal allowance of £12,570, no workplace pension, no student loan.

  • Gross pay: £35,000.00
  • Personal allowance in this run: £12,570.00
  • Taxable pay: £35,000.00 − £12,570 = £22,430.00
  • Income tax: 20% × £22,430 = £4,486.00
  • Employee National Insurance: 8% × (£35,000 − £12,570) = £1,794.40
  • Illustrated take-home: £28,719.60 a year (£2,393.30 a month · £552.30 a week)

You keep £28,719.60 of £35,000.00 in this illustration. That is not a budget until a payslip agrees.

How the figures were produced

The numbers above were calculated with the live js/tax.js engine that powers the homepage calculator — same personal allowance, same rest-of-UK income-tax bands, same NI primary threshold (£12,570), upper earnings limit (£50,270), main rate (8%) and upper rate (2%). Rounding is to the nearest penny in the usual way. Real PAYE National Insurance is assessed per pay period; this site models NI on an annual basis for a clear yearly picture. The method is also summarised on the methodology page.

Student loan and pension are optional inputs on the calculator. Turning either on reduces take-home further. A Plan 2 loan, for example, takes 9% of pay above its threshold; a salary-sacrifice pension reduces both taxable pay and NI-able pay. Neither is included in the £28,719.60 figure above so the baseline stays readable. See salary sacrifice versus net pay for how a 5% pension changes the packet on a related example.

What changes the packet from here

Three common forks move the illustration:

  • A pay rise toward £40,000 — still basic-rate and main NI until £50,270. What a £5,000 pay rise is worth shows the basic-rate maths, and What’s £40k take-home pay? works the next round number.
  • Scotland — income tax uses six Scottish bands; NI does not change. Scotland versus the rest of the UK compares the same salary on both stacks.
  • Pension or student loan — both leave less cash in the month even when the headline salary stays £35,000.00. Run those toggles on the calculator rather than guessing.

The £35k angle: the Scotland gap is pennies

£35,000 is close to where Scottish and rest-of-UK tax cross. Scottish income tax is £4,501.07 against £4,486.00, so Scotland takes £15.07 a year more: about £1.26 a month. The detail is on £35,000 in Scotland.

Quick answers

What is £35,000 a month after tax? £2,393.30 in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no pension or loan, and £2,392.04 in Scotland.

What hourly rate is £35,000? About £17.95 an hour gross on 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, or £14.73 an hour after tax and NI. See hourly pay to take-home.

How much of a £1,000 rise would I keep? £720.00 at this salary.

What this page does not decide

It does not say £35,000.00 is “enough”, that you should take a job, or that £28,719.60 is “your” take-home. Tax code, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and Scottish residence all sit outside this single illustration. Check any offer against a real payslip layout before you treat the monthly figure as a budget.
